\section{Examples and validation targets}
\label{sec:examples}

The repository contains several concrete instances that exercise distinct parts of the general construction.

\paragraph{Symmetric two-state chain.}
For the unit-rate alternating chain, the $n$-jump sector has mass $e^{-T}T^n/n!$. The sector masses sum to one, the jump-count law is Poisson, and the terminal distribution is the parity-filtered row of $\exp(TQ)$. This example checks normalization and the semigroup bridge against an explicit calculation.

\paragraph{Asymmetric reversible two-state quench.}
The rates $q(0,1)=2$ and $q(1,0)=1$ have equilibrium probabilities $(1/3,2/3)$. An explicit final energy quench has partition functions $Z_0=3$ and $Z_1=6$, hence $\Delta F=-\log 2$ at $\beta=1$. The formalization obtains exact two-atom work probabilities, the exact mean work, a strict second law, physical Crooks and Jarzynski relations, and an entropy-production integral fluctuation theorem. The same model is recovered as a one-window instance of the general driven construction.

\paragraph{Branching and history-dependent work.}
A three-state Y-shaped generator validates the general terminal-law theorem beyond two-state parity arguments. A separate two-window three-state protocol has positive-probability work atoms $0$ and $\log 2$. More strongly, positive-measure events realize both values while sharing the endpoint pair $(0,0)$ (\lean{work_not_ae_initialFinalFunction}). Thus the work is not determined by the overall initial and final states: the intermediate window-boundary history is essential, even though the work observable~\eqref{eq:work} itself reads only window-boundary states.

\paragraph{Interior states are not boundary functions.}
Retaining complete window paths serves a strictly stronger purpose than computing work: constructing and observing a single global real-time trajectory and proving path-level pushforward identities. This purpose is also witnessed by a theorem. For the symmetric two-state chain over one window of positive duration, the events that the trajectory started at $0$ returns to $0$ at the horizon while sitting at $0$, respectively at $1$, at the interior time $T/2$ both have positive probability, by the finite-dimensional atom formula~\eqref{eq:fdd-atom}. Consequently the interior-time state is not almost surely any function of the window's endpoint pair (\lean{trajectory_interior_not_ae_endpointFunction}). Window-boundary records therefore cannot replace the retained window paths: the real-time trajectory carries information beyond every boundary history.

These examples are not used to claim new stochastic-thermodynamic phenomena. They act as regression targets for normalization, branching, physical parameterization, work sign conventions, and the distinction between endpoint and path information.
