\section{Examples and validation targets}
\label{sec:examples}

The repository contains several concrete instances that exercise distinct parts of the general construction.

\paragraph{Symmetric two-state chain.}
For the unit-rate alternating chain, the $n$-jump sector has mass $e^{-T}T^n/n!$. The sector masses sum to one, the jump-count law is Poisson, and the terminal distribution is the parity-filtered row of $\exp(TQ)$. This example checks normalization and the semigroup bridge against an explicit calculation.

\paragraph{Asymmetric reversible two-state quench.}
At $\beta=1$ on states $\{0,1\}$, take
\[
  E_0(0)=0,\qquad E_0(1)=-\log 2,
  \qquad E_1(0)=E_1(1)=-\log 3,
\]
with rates $k(0,1)=2$ and $k(1,0)=1$. The partition functions are $Z_0=3$ and $Z_1=6$, hence $\Delta F=-\log 2$. The initial Gibbs law is $(1/3,2/3)$, and it is stationary under the rates $k(0,1)=2$, $k(1,0)=1$. Because the only energy switch occurs after the dynamical window, the work distribution is independent of the window duration:
\[
  \Pp_F(W=-\log 3)=\frac13,
  \qquad
  \Pp_F\left(W=-\log\frac32\right)=\frac23.
\]
Hence
\[
  \left\langle e^{-W}\right\rangle_F
  =\frac13\cdot3+\frac23\cdot\frac32
  =2=e^{-\Delta F},
  \qquad
  \langle W\rangle_F-\Delta F
  =\frac13\log\frac{32}{27}>0.
\]
This one-window example is thus a sign-convention test: a stationary Gibbs state followed by a single quench, rather than a nontrivial finite-time relaxation. The formalization also obtains physical Crooks and an entropy-production integral fluctuation theorem, and recovers the model as a one-window instance of the general driven construction.

\paragraph{Branching and history-dependent work.}
A three-state Y-shaped chain uses
\[
  Q=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    -2 & 1 & 1\\
     1 &-1 & 0\\
     1 & 0 &-1
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad 1\leftrightarrow0\leftrightarrow2.
\]
The branching at state $0$ is why jump-count parity arguments from the two-state alternating chain do not apply; this generator validates the general terminal-law theorem beyond that special case.

A separate two-window three-state protocol has energy landscapes
\[
  (0,0,0)\longrightarrow(0,\log 2,0)\longrightarrow(0,0,0).
\]
Its second-window generator doubles the rate out of the raised state, $k(1,0)=2$. The resting history $0\to0\to0$ has $W=0$. The history $0\to1\to0$ has $W=\log 2$, because the first switch raises state $1$ while the second switch occurs after the process has returned to state $0$. Both histories have the same endpoint pair $(0,0)$, and both have positive probability for positive window durations (\lean{work_zero_same_endpoints_event_pos}, \lean{work_log_two_same_endpoints_event_pos}). Consequently, the work is not determined almost surely by the overall initial and final states (\lean{work_not_ae_initialFinalFunction}): the intermediate window-boundary history is essential, even though the work observable~\eqref{eq:work} itself reads only window-boundary states.

\paragraph{Interior states are not boundary functions.}
Retaining complete window paths serves a strictly stronger purpose than computing work: constructing and observing a single global real-time trajectory and proving path-level pushforward identities. For the unit-rate symmetric two-state chain, the exact transition probabilities are
\[
  p_{00}(t)=\frac{1+e^{-2t}}{2},
  \qquad
  p_{01}(t)=\frac{1-e^{-2t}}{2},
\]
with $p_{10}=p_{01}$ and $p_{11}=p_{00}$. For $T>0$, under the law started from $X_0=0$, the two relevant joint masses are
\[
  \Pp_{T,0}(X_{T/2}=0,\,X_T=0)
  =p_{00}(T/2)^2>0,
\]
and
\[
  \Pp_{T,0}(X_{T/2}=1,\,X_T=0)
  =p_{01}(T/2)\,p_{10}(T/2)>0.
\]
These are the concrete finite-dimensional atoms behind the interior-time theorem. For $T>0$, within the endpoint event $(X_0,X_T)=(0,0)$, both midpoint values occur with positive probability, so the midpoint state is not almost surely equal to any measurable function of the endpoint pair (\lean{trajectory_interior_not_ae_endpointFunction}). Endpoint-only records are therefore insufficient for general path-level observables; the present construction retains complete window paths so that the global real-time trajectory and its finite-time observations remain available.

Together these examples act as regression targets for normalization, branching, physical parameterization, work sign conventions, and the distinction between endpoint and path information.
