\section{Piecewise-constant driving and global path laws}
\label{sec:driven}

\subsection{Marked window laws}

Consider $M$ protocol windows with durations $T_i$, energy landscapes
\[
  E_0,E_1,\ldots,E_M:\Omega\to\R,
\]
and generators $G_i$ for $i=0,\ldots,M-1$. Within window $i$, the generator is constant and the complete path is sampled from the normalized fixed-initial law already constructed. The next window starts at the recorded terminal state. The marked carrier therefore retains one complete \lean{FullPath} object per window rather than only its endpoints.

The reverse experiment begins from the final Gibbs state, traverses the windows in reverse order, and stores reversed complete paths. The development proves almost-sure boundary matching and fixed-horizon validity for both forward and reverse marked laws. A structurally boundary-consistent subtype is also available for deterministic reasoning.

The two protocols are time-mirror images of each other:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Forward:}\quad&
\rho_{E_0}
\xrightarrow{\;G_0,\,T_0\;}
\xrightarrow{\;E_0\to E_1\;}
\xrightarrow{\;G_1,\,T_1\;}
\xrightarrow{\;E_1\to E_2\;}
\cdots
\xrightarrow{\;G_{M-1},\,T_{M-1}\;}
\xrightarrow{\;E_{M-1}\to E_M\;},
\\[1mm]
\text{Reverse:}\quad&
\rho_{E_M}
\xrightarrow{\;E_M\to E_{M-1}\;}
\xrightarrow{\;G_{M-1},\,T_{M-1}\;}
\cdots
\xrightarrow{\;E_1\to E_0\;}
\xrightarrow{\;G_0,\,T_0\;}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For $M>0$, the forward experiment evolves under the constant generator $G_i$ on landscape $E_i$ for time $T_i$ and is quenched $E_i\to E_{i+1}$ at the end of the window; the reverse experiment applies the mirrored quench before the corresponding window. For $M=0$, both protocols degenerate to the equilibrium experiment with no evolution and zero work, and the theorems below hold trivially. Each stored reverse window path is then reversed in time. At the record level, reversal acts on a fixed-jump-count chart by reversing both the state sequence and the holding-time sequence (\lean{JumpPath.reverse}),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reversal}
  \Theta\bigl((x_0,\ldots,x_n),(\tau_0,\ldots,\tau_n)\bigr)
  = \bigl((x_n,\ldots,x_0),(\tau_n,\ldots,\tau_0)\bigr),
\end{equation}
so the reverse carrier used below is \emph{chronology-aligned}: it is charted in the forward time direction, not in the reverse experiment's own chronology. The time reversal used throughout is this record-level reversal; the state assigned at each jump time is fixed by the record convention, whereas the naive pointwise reversal $t\mapsto X_{T-t}$ of a right-continuous trajectory would not remain right-continuous at jump times. To keep this alignment visible, the aligned reverse law is written $\Pp_{R,\mathrm{align}}^{\mathrm{glob}}$ throughout.

\subsection{Measurable global concatenation}

The marked representation is convenient for recursive measure construction, but physical work should also be readable on a single real-time trajectory. The function \lean{concatenateWindows} restores chronological order and glues every window chart into one \lean{FullPath}. The seam holding intervals are merged by the lower-level concatenation operation. The formalization proves:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item measurability of the global concatenation map;
  \item addition of total holding times;
  \item preservation of validity over the summed horizon;
  \item right-continuous left and right trajectory gluing laws; and
  \item recovery of marked boundary states from the global trajectory almost surely.
\end{itemize}

The global laws are the pushforwards
\[
  \Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}
  =\Pp_F^{\mathrm{marked}}\circ C^{-1},
  \qquad
  \Pp_{R,\mathrm{align}}^{\mathrm{glob}}
  =\Pp_{R,\mathrm{align}}^{\mathrm{marked}}\circ C^{-1},
\]
where $C$ is chronological concatenation. Both are probability measures.

\subsection{Work and detailed balance}

Let $s_i=\sum_{j<i}T_j$ be the boundary times. Work is read from the global right-continuous trajectory at the end of each window:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:work}
  W(\gamma)=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}
  \left(E_{i+1}(X_{s_{i+1}})-E_i(X_{s_{i+1}})\right).
\end{equation}
This is the end-of-window switching-work (transition-then-quench) convention: within window $i$ the system evolves on landscape $E_i$, and the quench $E_i\to E_{i+1}$ is charged at the state occupied at the window boundary. For finite $\Omega$ and finitely many windows, this observable is bounded and therefore integrable under the constructed laws. The formalization proves that~\eqref{eq:work} agrees almost surely with the recursively defined marked work.

For inverse temperature $\beta\ne 0$, assume windowwise Gibbs detailed balance. Equivalently, rather than referring to the normalized Gibbs distribution, we use the division-free identity in unnormalized Boltzmann weights
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gibbs-db}
  e^{-\beta E_i(x)} k_i(x,y)
  = e^{-\beta E_i(y)} k_i(y,x)
  \qquad (x\ne y).
\end{equation}
``Windowwise'' means that each constant generator is reversible with respect to the Gibbs weight of the energy landscape used during its own window. This is deliberately weaker terminology than the ``local detailed balance'' of stochastic thermodynamics: no entropy-flow or bath decomposition beyond~\eqref{eq:gibbs-db} is assumed. Let
\[
  Z_i=\sum_{x\in\Omega}e^{-\beta E_i(x)},
  \qquad
  F_i=-\beta^{-1}\log Z_i,
  \qquad
  \Delta F=F_M-F_0.
\]
The path-density proof first establishes a window balance for mixtures of the actual fixed-initial laws, then lifts it through the recursive marked construction, and finally maps it through global concatenation.

\subsection{Global fluctuation relations}

For a measure $P$ and a measurable weight $q\ge 0$, the reweighted measure is
\[
  \bigl(P.\operatorname{withDensity}(q)\bigr)(A)
  = \int_A q(\gamma)\,P(\mathrm{d}\gamma).
\]
The central result of the development can now be stated as one theorem whose hypotheses are exactly those of \lean{global_crooks_of_gibbsDetailedBalance}.

\begin{theorem}[Global Crooks relation]
\label{thm:main}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item $\Omega$ is a nonempty finite state space;
  \item the protocol has finitely many windows $M$ with durations $T_i\ge 0$;
  \item each window carries a finite jump generator $G_i$ satisfying the windowwise Gibbs detailed balance~\eqref{eq:gibbs-db} for its own landscape $E_i$;
  \item $\beta\ne 0$;
  \item the forward experiment starts from the initial Gibbs state $e^{-\beta E_0}/Z_0$ and the reverse experiment starts from the final Gibbs state $e^{-\beta E_M}/Z_M$;
  \item work uses the transition-then-quench convention~\eqref{eq:work}, and the reverse law is the chronology-aligned pushforward $\Pp_{R,\mathrm{align}}^{\mathrm{glob}}$ of the reverse experiment's window records under record reversal~\eqref{eq:reversal} and concatenation.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:global-crooks}
  \Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}.\operatorname{withDensity}
  \left(e^{-\beta W}\right)
  = e^{-\beta\Delta F}\,\Pp_{R,\mathrm{align}}^{\mathrm{glob}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

In the conventional physicists' differential notation, identity~\eqref{eq:global-crooks} reads
\[
  e^{-\beta W(\gamma)}\,\Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}(\mathrm{d}\gamma)
  = e^{-\beta\Delta F}\,\Pp_{R,\mathrm{align}}^{\mathrm{glob}}(\mathrm{d}\gamma).
\]
The formal statement is the \lean{withDensity} measure identity, not a pointwise density ratio; this division-free form avoids ratios on zero-probability trajectories.

\begin{corollary}[Jarzynski equality]
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, evaluating~\eqref{eq:global-crooks} on the whole path space yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jarzynski}
  \E_F\left[e^{-\beta W}\right]=e^{-\beta\Delta F}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Average-work second law]
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with $\beta>0$, Jensen's inequality and integrability of $W$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:second-law}
  \Delta F\le \E_F[W].
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The Crooks and Jarzynski identities are formal theorems for any $\beta\ne 0$; the thermodynamic reading of $\Delta F$ and the second law require $\beta>0$.

The development also maps~\eqref{eq:global-crooks} to the work coordinate, and it does so at the level of measures first, consistent with the design decision of Section~\ref{sec:architecture}. Recall that $\Pp_{R,\mathrm{align}}^{\mathrm{glob}}$ is charted in the forward chronology, so the aligned work coordinate $W$ of~\eqref{eq:work} is available on it; the reverse experiment's own work observable is by definition the negated aligned coordinate, $W_R=-W$ (\lean{globalReverseWork}), and the development proves that its law agrees with the recursively defined marked reverse work. Writing
\[
  \mu_F=W_*\,\Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}},
  \qquad
  \mu_R=(W_R)_*\,\Pp_{R,\mathrm{align}}^{\mathrm{glob}},
\]
the pushforward of~\eqref{eq:global-crooks} is the measure-level work-distribution relation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:work-crooks}
  \mu_F.\operatorname{withDensity}\bigl(w\mapsto e^{-\beta w}\bigr)
  = e^{-\beta\Delta F}\,(-\mathrm{id})_*\,\mu_R
\end{equation}
(\lean{global_work_distribution_crooks_reverseWork_of_gibbsDetailedBalance}). Because $\Omega$ and $M$ are finite, $W$ has finite range, and evaluating~\eqref{eq:work-crooks} on singletons recovers the conventional atomwise statement
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atom-crooks}
  \Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}(W=w)
  =e^{\beta(w-\Delta F)}\,\Pp_{R,\mathrm{align}}^{\mathrm{glob}}(W_R=-w).
\end{equation}
All of these theorems are stated on the global pushforward laws constructed from the generators, rather than on abstract work distributions supplied as assumptions.
