\section{Piecewise-constant driving and global path laws}
\label{sec:driven}

\subsection{Marked window laws}

Consider $M$ protocol windows with durations $T_i$, energy landscapes
\[
  E_0,E_1,\ldots,E_M:\Omega\to\R,
\]
and generators $G_i$ for $i=0,\ldots,M-1$. Within window $i$, the generator is constant and the complete path is sampled from the normalized fixed-initial law already constructed. The next window starts at the recorded terminal state. The marked carrier therefore retains one complete \lean{FullPath} object per window rather than only its endpoints.

The reverse experiment begins from the final Gibbs state, traverses the windows in reverse order, and stores reversed complete paths. The development proves almost-sure boundary matching and fixed-horizon validity for both forward and reverse marked laws. A structurally boundary-consistent subtype is also available for deterministic reasoning.

\subsection{Measurable global concatenation}

The marked representation is convenient for recursive measure construction, but physical work should also be readable on a single real-time trajectory. The function \lean{concatenateWindows} restores chronological order and glues every window chart into one \lean{FullPath}. The seam holding intervals are merged by the lower-level concatenation operation. The formalization proves:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item measurability of the global concatenation map;
  \item addition of total holding times;
  \item preservation of validity over the summed horizon;
  \item right-continuous left and right trajectory gluing laws; and
  \item recovery of marked boundary states from the global trajectory almost surely.
\end{itemize}

The global laws are the pushforwards
\[
  \Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}
  =\Pp_F^{\mathrm{marked}}\circ C^{-1},
  \qquad
  \Pp_R^{\mathrm{glob}}
  =\Pp_R^{\mathrm{marked}}\circ C^{-1},
\]
where $C$ is chronological concatenation. Both are probability measures.

\subsection{Work and detailed balance}

Let $s_i=\sum_{j<i}T_j$ be the boundary times. Work is read from the global right-continuous trajectory at the end of each window:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:work}
  W(\gamma)=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}
  \left(E_{i+1}(X_{s_{i+1}})-E_i(X_{s_{i+1}})\right).
\end{equation}
For finite $\Omega$, this observable is bounded and therefore integrable under the constructed laws. The formalization proves that~\eqref{eq:work} agrees almost surely with the recursively defined marked work.

For inverse temperature $\beta\ne 0$, assume division-free local Gibbs detailed balance in every window, equivalently
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gibbs-db}
  e^{-\beta E_i(x)} k_i(x,y)
  = e^{-\beta E_i(y)} k_i(y,x)
  \qquad (x\ne y).
\end{equation}
Let
\[
  Z_i=\sum_{x\in\Omega}e^{-\beta E_i(x)},
  \qquad
  F_i=-\beta^{-1}\log Z_i,
  \qquad
  \Delta F=F_M-F_0.
\]
The path-density proof first establishes a window balance for mixtures of the actual fixed-initial laws, then lifts it through the recursive marked construction, and finally maps it through global concatenation.

\subsection{Global fluctuation relations}

The relations below are stated for the forward global law started from the initial Gibbs state $e^{-\beta E_0}/Z_0$ and the reverse global law started from the final Gibbs state $e^{-\beta E_M}/Z_M$; these initial distributions are part of the formal theorem statements. The central measure identity on the global chart is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:global-crooks}
  \Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}.\operatorname{withDensity}
  \left(e^{-\beta W}\right)
  = e^{-\beta\Delta F}\,\Pp_R^{\mathrm{glob}}.
\end{equation}
This division-free form avoids ratios on zero-probability trajectories. Evaluating~\eqref{eq:global-crooks} on the whole path space yields Jarzynski's equality
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jarzynski}
  \E_F\left[e^{-\beta W}\right]=e^{-\beta\Delta F}.
\end{equation}
For $\beta>0$, Jensen's inequality and integrability of $W$ give the average-work second law
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:second-law}
  \Delta F\le \E_F[W].
\end{equation}

The development also maps~\eqref{eq:global-crooks} to the work coordinate. On the chronology-aligned global reverse chart, the intrinsic reverse-experiment work is defined by $W_R=-W$. The conventional atomwise statement is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atom-crooks}
  \Pp_F(W=w)
  =e^{\beta(w-\Delta F)}\Pp_R(W_R=-w).
\end{equation}
All of these theorems are stated on the global pushforward laws constructed from the generators, rather than on abstract work distributions supplied as assumptions.
