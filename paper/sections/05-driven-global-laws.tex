\section{Piecewise-constant driving and global path laws}
\label{sec:driven}

\subsection{Marked window laws}

Consider $M$ protocol windows with durations $T_i$, energy landscapes
\[
  E_0,E_1,\ldots,E_M:\Omega\to\R,
\]
and generators $G_i$ for $i=0,\ldots,M-1$. Within window $i$, the generator is constant and the complete path is sampled from the normalized fixed-initial law already constructed. The next window starts at the recorded terminal state. The marked carrier therefore retains one complete \lean{FullPath} object per window rather than only its endpoints.

The reverse experiment begins from the final Gibbs state, traverses the windows in reverse order, and samples complete window paths in its own chronology; each path is reversed once, before it is stored, as made precise below. The development proves almost-sure boundary matching and fixed-horizon validity for both forward and reverse marked laws. A structurally boundary-consistent subtype is also available for deterministic reasoning.

The two protocols are time-mirror images of each other:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Forward:}\quad&
\rho_{E_0}
\xrightarrow{\;G_0,\,T_0\;}
\xrightarrow{\;E_0\to E_1\;}
\xrightarrow{\;G_1,\,T_1\;}
\xrightarrow{\;E_1\to E_2\;}
\cdots
\xrightarrow{\;G_{M-1},\,T_{M-1}\;}
\xrightarrow{\;E_{M-1}\to E_M\;},
\\[1mm]
\text{Reverse:}\quad&
\rho_{E_M}
\xrightarrow{\;E_M\to E_{M-1}\;}
\xrightarrow{\;G_{M-1},\,T_{M-1}\;}
\cdots
\xrightarrow{\;E_1\to E_0\;}
\xrightarrow{\;G_0,\,T_0\;}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The forward protocol stops immediately after the final switch to $E_M$; the reverse experiment starts in the Gibbs state of $E_M$ and performs the mirrored switch before its first dynamical window.

For $M>0$, the forward experiment evolves under the constant generator $G_i$ on landscape $E_i$ for time $T_i$ and is quenched $E_i\to E_{i+1}$ at the end of the window; the reverse experiment applies the mirrored quench before the corresponding window. For $M=0$, both protocols degenerate to the equilibrium experiment with no evolution and zero work, and the theorems below hold trivially.

Let $\Pp_R$ denote the law of the reverse protocol in its own chronology, and let $\Pp_R^{\Theta}$ denote its chronology-aligned image on the common path carrier. For a c\`adl\`ag trajectory $X$ on a horizon $[0,\tau]$ and a kinematic time-reversal involution $\vartheta$, the standard time reversal is
\[
  (\Theta X)_t=\vartheta X_{(\tau-t)-}\quad(0\le t<\tau),
  \qquad
  (\Theta X)_\tau=\vartheta X_0 .
\]
In the present work all state variables are even, so $\vartheta=\mathrm{id}$. The left-limit convention is what keeps the reversed trajectory right-continuous: the naive pointwise reversal $t\mapsto X_{\tau-t}$ would fail right continuity at jump times. On a fixed-jump-count chart, the corresponding reversal of finite jump records is (\lean{JumpPath.reverse})
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reversal}
  \Theta\bigl((x_0,\ldots,x_n),(\tau_0,\ldots,\tau_n)\bigr)
  = \bigl((x_n,\ldots,x_0),(\tau_n,\ldots,\tau_0)\bigr),
\end{equation}
which fixes the state assigned at each jump time in accordance with that left-limit convention. The quench at the beginning of the reverse experiment is not an additional operation; it is the exact time mirror of the final quench of the forward protocol.

In the formal construction, each reverse window path is transformed by $\Theta$ exactly once, before it is stored on the common forward-aligned marked carrier (\lean{reverseWindowKernel}); the marked reverse law is therefore already chronology-aligned, and the global reverse law $\Pp_R^{\Theta}$ is obtained from it by chronological concatenation. In this way $\Pp_R^{\Theta}$ plays the role of the time-reversed image $\Theta_*\Pp_R$ of the reverse-protocol law; it is called the aligned reverse law in the formal development, and it is charted in the forward time direction, not in the reverse experiment's own chronology.

\subsection{Measurable global concatenation}

The marked representation is convenient for recursive measure construction, but physical work should also be readable on a single real-time trajectory. The function \lean{concatenateWindows} restores chronological order and glues every window chart into one \lean{FullPath}. The seam holding intervals are merged by the lower-level concatenation operation. The formalization proves:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item measurability of the global concatenation map;
  \item addition of total holding times;
  \item preservation of validity over the summed horizon;
  \item right-continuous left and right trajectory gluing laws; and
  \item recovery of marked boundary states from the global trajectory almost surely.
\end{itemize}

The global laws are the pushforwards
\[
  \Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}
  =\Pp_F^{\mathrm{marked}}\circ C^{-1},
  \qquad
  \Pp_R^{\Theta}
  =\Pp_R^{\Theta,\mathrm{marked}}\circ C^{-1},
\]
where $C$ is chronological concatenation. Both are probability measures.

\subsection{Work and detailed balance}

Let $s_i=\sum_{j<i}T_j$ be the boundary times. Work is read from the global right-continuous trajectory at the end of each window:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:work}
  W(\gamma)=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}
  \left(E_{i+1}(X_{s_{i+1}})-E_i(X_{s_{i+1}})\right).
\end{equation}
During window $i$, the system evolves for time $T_i$ under $G_i$ at the fixed energy landscape $E_i$. At the end of the window, the control parameter is switched instantaneously from $E_i$ to $E_{i+1}$ while the state is held fixed. A stochastic jump at the deterministically prescribed switching time has probability zero, so $X_{s_{i+1}^-}=X_{s_{i+1}}$ almost surely, and~\eqref{eq:work} is the usual inclusive switching work, charged at the state occupied at the window boundary, with positive sign for work done on the system. We refer to this as the end-of-window quench convention; ``transition-then-quench'' is the name used in the formal development. For finite $\Omega$ and finitely many windows, this observable is bounded and therefore integrable under the constructed laws. The formalization proves that~\eqref{eq:work} agrees almost surely with the recursively defined marked work.

In the usual single-bath interpretation, jumps within a window change the system energy at fixed control parameter and are associated with heat exchange, whereas the instantaneous switches contribute work \parencite{Sekimoto2010,Seifert2012}. Heat and a trajectory-level first law are not formalized in the present development.

Fix an inverse temperature $\beta\ne 0$. The formal identities below are proved for every $\beta\ne0$; the standard thermal interpretation assumes $\beta>0$, and negative $\beta$ may be read as a formal parameter. During each fixed-control window, the time-homogeneous generator $G_i$ obeys ordinary detailed balance with respect to the Gibbs distribution of the current landscape,
\[
  \pi_i(x)=Z_i^{-1}e^{-\beta E_i(x)},
  \qquad
  \pi_i(x)\,k_i(x,y)=\pi_i(y)\,k_i(y,x).
\]
On every edge with nonvanishing rates this is the familiar rate-ratio form
\[
  \log\frac{k_i(x,y)}{k_i(y,x)}
  =-\beta\bigl[E_i(y)-E_i(x)\bigr],
\]
while, after cancelling $Z_i$, the unnormalized identity used in Lean is the division-free form that also covers absent edges:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gibbs-db}
  e^{-\beta E_i(x)} k_i(x,y)
  = e^{-\beta E_i(y)} k_i(y,x)
  \qquad (x\ne y).
\end{equation}
This division-free form is the hypothesis \lean{FiniteJumpGenerator.IsGibbsDetailedBalance}; the formal development calls it windowwise Gibbs detailed balance. In stochastic-thermodynamic language, condition~\eqref{eq:gibbs-db} is the one-reservoir, energy-only specialization of local detailed balance \parencite{Maes2021}; no reservoir decomposition or reservoir-resolved entropy flow is modeled. Detailed balance is a property of the generator $G_i$: it makes $\pi_i$ the stationary reference distribution of the window, and it does not imply that the actual state distribution equals $\pi_i$ during the finite window duration $T_i$. Since $G_i$ is reversible with respect to $\pi_i$, the reverse protocol uses the same window generator $G_i$, with the windows traversed in the opposite order. Let
\[
  Z_i=\sum_{x\in\Omega}e^{-\beta E_i(x)},
  \qquad
  F_i=-\beta^{-1}\log Z_i,
  \qquad
  \Delta F=F_M-F_0.
\]
Here $F_M$ is the equilibrium free energy associated with the final landscape $E_M$; the actual forward ensemble need not be equilibrated at the end of the protocol. It is the reverse experiment that prepares the Gibbs distribution of $E_M$, before its initial mirrored quench.

The physical content of the balance condition can be seen directly at the trajectory level. For a forward trajectory $\gamma$ and its conjugate reverse trajectory, the exponential survival factors of the sector density~\eqref{eq:sector-density} cancel, because the two trajectories visit the same states for the same holding times. Every jump $x\to y$ in window $i$ contributes the rate ratio $\log[k_i(x,y)/k_i(y,x)]=-\beta[E_i(y)-E_i(x)]$, and these energy increments telescope against the Gibbs boundary weights: heuristically,
\[
  \log\frac{\dd\Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}}{\dd\Pp_R^{\Theta}}(\gamma)
  = \log\frac{\pi_0(x_0)}{\pi_M(x_\tau)}
  +\sum_{\mathrm{jumps}}
   \log\frac{k_i(x^-,x^+)}{k_i(x^+,x^-)}
  = \beta\bigl[W(\gamma)-\Delta F\bigr],
\]
since the total energy change along the trajectory splits into the jump contributions and the switching work~\eqref{eq:work}. This is the familiar trajectory-level cancellation of stochastic thermodynamics \parencite{Crooks1999,Seifert2012,Maes2021}; the theorem below is its measure-theoretic realization on the constructed path law, stated without dividing by the probability of an individual trajectory. The path-density proof establishes exactly this identity in division-free form: it first proves a window balance for mixtures of the actual fixed-initial laws, then lifts it through the recursive marked construction, and finally maps it through global concatenation.

\subsection{Global fluctuation relations}

For a measure $P$ and a measurable weight $q\ge 0$, the reweighted measure is
\[
  \bigl(P.\operatorname{withDensity}(q)\bigr)(A)
  = \int_A q(\gamma)\,P(\mathrm{d}\gamma).
\]
The central result of the development can now be stated as one theorem whose hypotheses are exactly those of \lean{global_crooks_of_gibbsDetailedBalance}.

\begin{theorem}[Path-space Crooks relation]
\label{thm:main}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item $\Omega$ is a nonempty finite state space;
  \item the protocol has finitely many windows $M$ with durations $T_i\ge 0$;
  \item each window carries a finite jump generator $G_i$ satisfying the windowwise Gibbs detailed balance~\eqref{eq:gibbs-db} for its own landscape $E_i$;
  \item $\beta\ne 0$;
  \item the forward experiment starts from the initial Gibbs state $e^{-\beta E_0}/Z_0$ and the reverse experiment starts from the final Gibbs state $e^{-\beta E_M}/Z_M$;
  \item work uses the end-of-window quench convention~\eqref{eq:work}, and the reverse law is the chronology-aligned pushforward $\Pp_R^{\Theta}$ of the reverse experiment's window records under record reversal~\eqref{eq:reversal} and concatenation.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:global-crooks}
  e^{-\beta W(\gamma)}\,\Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}(\dd\gamma)
  =e^{-\beta\Delta F}\,\Pp_R^{\Theta}(\dd\gamma).
\end{equation}
Whenever ratio notation is legitimate, this is equivalently
\begin{equation}
  \log\frac{\dd\Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}}{\dd\Pp_R^{\Theta}}(\gamma)
  =\beta\bigl(W(\gamma)-\Delta F\bigr).
\end{equation}
The Lean theorem states the first, division-free measure identity, which remains meaningful without dividing by the probability of an individual trajectory:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:global-crooks-lean}
  \Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}.\operatorname{withDensity}
  \left(e^{-\beta W}\right)
  = e^{-\beta\Delta F}\,\Pp_R^{\Theta}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Defining the dissipated work $W_{\mathrm{diss}}=W-\Delta F$, the path-space irreversibility variable is $\Sigma=\beta W_{\mathrm{diss}}$ \parencite{Seifert2012,VandenBroeckEsposito2015}. Jarzynski's equality and the mean-work inequality are respectively the exponential and first-moment consequences of this same path-measure identity. The machine-checked Crooks and Jarzynski identities hold for every $\beta\ne0$; the average-work inequality is stated for $\beta>0$. No irreducibility, uniqueness-of-equilibrium, or long-window equilibration assumption is required.

For $\beta>0$, averaging the logarithmic density under the forward law gives the standard information-theoretic reading
\[
  D\!\left(\Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}\,\middle\|\,\Pp_R^{\Theta}\right)
  = \beta\bigl(\langle W\rangle_F-\Delta F\bigr):
\]
the mean dissipated work is the path-space distinguishability of the forward and reverse experiments \parencite{KawaiParrondoVandenBroeck2007}. This is a standard consequence of~\eqref{eq:global-crooks-lean}, stated here as interpretation; it is not part of the mechanized development.

\begin{corollary}[Jarzynski equality]
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, evaluating~\eqref{eq:global-crooks-lean} on the whole path space yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jarzynski}
  \E_F\left[e^{-\beta W}\right]=e^{-\beta\Delta F}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Average-work second law]
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with $\beta>0$, Jensen's inequality and integrability of $W$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:second-law}
  \Delta F\le \E_F[W].
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The development also maps~\eqref{eq:global-crooks-lean} to the work coordinate, and it does so at the level of measures first, consistent with the design decision of Section~\ref{sec:architecture}. Recall that $\Pp_R^{\Theta}$ is charted in the forward chronology, so the aligned work coordinate $W$ of~\eqref{eq:work} is available on it. In the reverse experiment's own chronology, with window boundary times $\tilde s_j$ and trajectory $\tilde X$, the reverse protocol quenches $E_{M-j}\to E_{M-j-1}$ before its $j$-th window, so its intrinsic switching work is
\[
  W_R(\tilde\gamma)
  =\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}
  \left[
    E_{M-j-1}\bigl(\tilde X_{\tilde s_j}\bigr)
    -E_{M-j}\bigl(\tilde X_{\tilde s_j}\bigr)
  \right].
\]
We use the same symbol $W_R$ for the transport of this intrinsic reverse-chronology observable to the aligned carrier. Under record reversal and chronology alignment, it equals the negated aligned coordinate, $W_R=-W$, an identity proved pointwise in the development (\lean{reverseWork_eq_neg}); the law of $W_R$ agrees with the recursively defined marked reverse work. Writing
\[
  \mu_F=W_*\,\Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}},
  \qquad
  \mu_R=(W_R)_*\,\Pp_R^{\Theta},
\]
the pushforward of~\eqref{eq:global-crooks-lean} is the measure-level Crooks work-distribution relation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:work-crooks}
  \mu_F.\operatorname{withDensity}\bigl(w\mapsto e^{-\beta w}\bigr)
  = e^{-\beta\Delta F}\,(-\mathrm{id})_*\,\mu_R
\end{equation}
(\lean{global_work_distribution_crooks_reverseWork_of_gibbsDetailedBalance}). Because $\Omega$ and $M$ are finite, $W$ has finite range, and evaluating~\eqref{eq:work-crooks} on singletons recovers the conventional atomwise statement
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atom-crooks}
  \Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}(W=w)
  =e^{\beta(w-\Delta F)}\,\Pp_R^{\Theta}(W_R=-w).
\end{equation}
All of these theorems are stated on the global pushforward laws constructed from the generators, rather than on abstract work distributions supplied as assumptions.
