\section{Introduction}

Are the trajectories entering Crooks' fluctuation relation the same stochastic process whose one-time probabilities solve the master equation? For a standard continuous-time Markov chain the answer is, of course, yes: stochastic thermodynamics routinely moves between a rate generator, a normalized trajectory ensemble, and forward and reverse trajectory likelihoods, and their compatibility is standard in informal calculations. The contribution of this paper is to establish that compatibility as one end-to-end, machine-checked construction: for finite-state, finite-time, stepwise-driven jump processes, all three descriptions are proved to refer to the same constructed probability law.

Fluctuation relations connect nonequilibrium trajectory statistics to equilibrium free-energy differences. Jarzynski's equality and Crooks' fluctuation relation are classical results, with formulations for Markovian systems, master equations, and path ensembles \parencite{Jarzynski1997PRL,Jarzynski1997PRE,Crooks1998,Crooks1999,Crooks2000}. Continuous-time Markov chains and finite-state jump processes are likewise standard: a conservative generator determines exponential holding times, an embedded jump chain away from absorbing states, a transition semigroup $P_t=\exp(tQ)$, and explicit finite-record path densities \parencite{Norris1997,Gardiner2009,RaoTeh2013}.
What is less automatic in a proof assistant is that the objects used at different levels of the usual argument are actually the same probability law. A sector-mass calculation does not by itself identify a pushforward of a constructed path measure. A matrix exponential defined independently of the path space does not by itself describe the terminal or finite-dimensional marginals of that measure. A list of window laws does not by itself yield a measurable global real-time trajectory. Finally, an abstract detailed fluctuation identity does not by itself show that concrete forward and reverse laws generated by physical dynamics satisfy it.

This paper presents an end-to-end mechanization of that connection. The formal development begins with nonnegative jump rates on a finite state space, constructs a fixed-horizon probability measure on the disjoint union of all finite jump records, identifies the actual terminal and finite-dimensional pushforwards with the matrix-exponential semigroup, concatenates finitely many piecewise-constant windows into one real-time path, and proves physical fluctuation relations on the resulting global laws.

The main contributions are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\arabic*)]
  \item We construct forward and reverse global path laws for a finite stepwise protocol directly from rates, energies, and durations, and prove the Crooks and Jarzynski relations and, for positive inverse temperature, the second-law inequality on that constructed global path space.

  \item Underlying the protocol, we construct the finite-horizon jump-trajectory measure of each window as a sum over jump-count sectors and prove its normalization, equivalently finite-horizon non-explosion.

  \item We identify the terminal law and all finite-dimensional distributions of that same measure, at nondecreasing observation times, with the Markov semigroup $\exp(tQ)$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Main physical result.}
For a finite state space and finitely many constant-control windows separated by instantaneous quenches, assume that each window generator is reversible with respect to the Gibbs distribution of its own energy landscape at a common inverse temperature $\beta>0$. Starting the forward experiment from the initial Gibbs state and the reverse experiment from the final Gibbs state, the constructed global laws are mutually absolutely continuous and satisfy
\[
  \frac{\dd\Pp_F^{\mathrm{glob}}}{\dd\Pp_R^{\Theta}}(\gamma)
  =\exp\!\bigl[\beta\bigl(W(\gamma)-\Delta F\bigr)\bigr],
\]
where $W$ is the inclusive switching work and $\Delta F$ the equilibrium free-energy difference of the initial and final landscapes (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}). No irreducibility, uniqueness-of-equilibrium, long-window equilibration, or smooth-protocol limit is assumed. The normalization of the trajectory law and its identification with $\exp(tQ)$ are the two enabling results behind this theorem.

\paragraph{Scope and novelty.}
The verified result concerns a finite state space, a finite total time, and finitely many stepwise changes of the energy landscape. Each dynamical window is governed by a constant, single-temperature generator that is reversible with respect to the instantaneous Gibbs distribution, and time reversal acts trivially on the states. The result does not cover smooth calendar-time dependence or a step-to-smooth refinement limit, multiple reservoirs, nonconservative steady-state forcing, odd time-reversal variables, infinite-time path laws, or diffusions. Mechanized Markov-process theory predates this work, including Isabelle/HOL developments and recent Lean infrastructure \parencite{Hoelzl2017,AFPMarkovModels,Marion2025,Ripple2026}; concurrent Lean work also studies consequences of an assumed detailed fluctuation theorem \parencite{PhysicsAIDFT}.
