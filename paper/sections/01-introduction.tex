\section{Introduction}

Fluctuation relations connect nonequilibrium trajectory statistics to equilibrium free-energy differences. Jarzynski's equality and Crooks' fluctuation relation are classical results, with formulations for Markovian systems, master equations, and path ensembles \parencite{Jarzynski1997PRL,Jarzynski1997PRE,Crooks1998,Crooks1999,Crooks2000}. Continuous-time Markov chains and finite-state jump processes are likewise standard: a conservative generator determines exponential holding times, an embedded jump chain, a transition semigroup $P_t=\exp(tQ)$, and explicit finite-record path densities \parencite{Norris1997,RaoTeh2013}. The mathematical identities are not new.

What is less automatic in a proof assistant is that the objects used at different levels of the usual argument are actually the same probability law. A sector-mass calculation does not by itself identify a pushforward of a constructed path measure. A matrix exponential defined independently of the path space does not by itself describe the terminal or finite-dimensional marginals of that measure. A list of window laws does not by itself yield a measurable global real-time trajectory. Finally, an abstract detailed fluctuation identity does not by itself show that concrete forward and reverse laws generated by physical dynamics satisfy it.

This paper presents an end-to-end mechanization of that connection. The formal development begins with nonnegative jump rates on a finite state space, constructs a fixed-horizon probability measure on the disjoint union of all finite jump records, identifies the actual terminal and finite-dimensional pushforwards with the matrix-exponential semigroup, concatenates finitely many piecewise-constant windows into one real-time path, and proves physical fluctuation relations on the resulting global laws.

The main contributions are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\arabic*)]
  \item We construct every fixed-jump-count sector from a nonzero free-coordinate simplex reference, prove a Poisson-type bound on the unfinished-arrival mass, and derive normalization of the countable sector sum. This is a finite-horizon non-explosion theorem at the level of the constructed measure.

  \item We prove that the terminal-state pushforward of that measure is the corresponding row of $\exp(TQ)$. The proof connects a first-jump renewal equation for the path construction to a uniqueness theorem for the matrix-exponential solution. The resulting terminal laws form a Mathlib Markov kernel satisfying Chapman--Kolmogorov.

  \item We identify every finite-dimensional marginal obtained by sampling the real-time trajectory at monotone observation times with the chronological path measure of the transition kernels. Consequently, every finite-dimensional atom is a product of matrix-exponential entries.

  \item We define marked forward and reverse laws for finitely many piecewise-constant windows, prove measurable chronological concatenation into a single global chart, read physical work from that chart at window boundaries, and transport Crooks, Jarzynski, the average-work second law, and work-distribution relations to the global laws.

  \item We expose a compact paper-to-code theorem spine and an auditable artifact. The repository contains no \lean{sorry} placeholders or custom axiom declarations, and its continuous-integration workflow prints the kernel axioms used by the headline theorems.
\end{enumerate}

The repository also contains a broader discrete-time measure-theoretic development, non-atomic Gaussian and Metropolis--Hastings examples, two-state calculations, and a refinement theorem for two discrete work conventions. Those components support and test the library, but the present paper deliberately focuses on the continuous-time chain
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \text{generator}
    &\longrightarrow \text{normalized fixed-horizon path law} \\
    &\longrightarrow \exp(TQ)\text{ terminal and FDD laws} \\
    &\longrightarrow \text{global driven path law}
      \longrightarrow \text{Crooks--Jarzynski relations}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\paragraph{Scope and novelty.}
We do not claim the first mechanization of continuous-time Markov chains, trajectory measures, non-explosion, thermodynamics, or fluctuation theorems. Mechanized Markov-process theory predates this work, including Isabelle/HOL developments and recent Lean infrastructure \parencite{Hoelzl2017,AFPMarkovModels,Marion2025,Ripple2026}. Concurrent Lean work also studies consequences of an assumed detailed fluctuation theorem \parencite{PhysicsAIDFT}. Our claim is the verified end-to-end connection for finite-state, finite-horizon jump processes and finitely many piecewise-constant protocol windows. Section~\ref{sec:related} makes this positioning precise.
