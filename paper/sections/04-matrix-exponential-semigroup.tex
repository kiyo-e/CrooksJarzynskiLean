\section{From the constructed law to the matrix-exponential semigroup}
\label{sec:semigroup}

\subsection{The terminal marginal is a genuine pushforward}

For arithmetic convenience, the development first defines the sector mass that ends at $y$ and sums it over $n$ to obtain a transition mass $p_T(x,y)$. A separate bridge theorem proves that this sum is the terminal marginal of the actual path law:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:terminal-push}
  (\Pp_{T,x}\circ X_T^{-1})(\{y\})=p_T(x,y),
\end{equation}
where $X_T$ is the terminal-state coordinate. This step matters in a proof assistant: without it, a calculation about $p_T$ would not automatically be a statement about \lean{pathLawFrom}.

\subsection{Renewal equation and uniqueness}

The path construction satisfies a first-jump renewal equation. In physical time it takes the familiar form
\[
  p_t(x,y)
  = \delta_{xy}\,e^{-\lambda(x)t}
  + \int_0^{t} e^{-\lambda(x)s}
    \sum_{z\in\Omega} k(x,z)\, p_{t-s}(z,y)\,\mathrm{d}s :
\]
either the path survives without jumping, or it holds for a time $s$, jumps to a successor $z$, and continues over the remaining time. The Lean proof uses the reparametrization $t=T\rho$ with a residual fraction $\rho\in[0,1]$ of a fixed horizon, in order to keep the free-simplex coordinates of Section~\ref{sec:setting} fixed: writing $p_{T,\rho}(x,y)$ for the transition mass over the residual time $T\rho$ (\lean{transitionMassAt}), peeling the first free simplex coordinate leaves the remaining coordinates in a simplex at fraction $\rho-u_0$ without rescaling the chart. After summing over the first successor state, the renewal equation reads (\lean{transitionMassAt_renewal})
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:renewal}
\begin{aligned}
  p_{T,\rho}(x,y)
  ={}& \mathbf{1}_{\{x=y\}}\, e^{-T\rho\,\lambda(x)}\\
  &+ T\int_0^{\rho} e^{-Tu\,\lambda(x)}
    \sum_{z\in\Omega} k(x,z)\, p_{T,\rho-u}(z,y)\,\mathrm{d}u:
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
the same first-jump alternative as above, expressed in the residual fraction.

On the analytic side, the development proves that
\[
  \rho\longmapsto \exp((T\rho)Q)(x,y)
\]
is a continuous solution of the same equation~\eqref{eq:renewal} and that bounded continuous solutions are unique: the difference $d$ of two bounded solutions satisfies a homogeneous version of~\eqref{eq:renewal}, and iterating that inequality $n$ times bounds it by
\[
  \lvert d(\rho)\rvert \le C\,\frac{(RT\rho)^n}{n!}
  \longrightarrow 0,
\]
where $R$ caps the escape rates. Matching the path-side solution to the matrix-exponential solution gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:terminal-exp}
  \operatorname{toReal}\bigl((\Pp_{T,x}\circ X_T^{-1})(\{y\})\bigr)
  = \exp(TQ)(x,y),
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{toReal}$ reads the finite singleton mass, an extended nonnegative real, as a real number; Section~\ref{sec:architecture} discusses this coercion and the kernel conventions.

The transition mass is bounded by one and continuous in $\rho$, so the uniqueness theorem applies and the solutions coincide. Combining the identification with the bridge~\eqref{eq:terminal-push} transfers the equality to the constructed law.

The matrix exponential first supplies the familiar semigroup identity
\[
  e^{(S+T)Q}=e^{SQ}e^{TQ}.
\]
The terminal laws are then packaged as a transition kernel satisfying the Chapman--Kolmogorov relation (a Mathlib Markov kernel)
\[
  K_T(x,\cdot)=\Pp_{T,x}\circ X_T^{-1}.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:terminal-exp} turns the matrix-exponential semigroup identity into Chapman--Kolmogorov:
\[
  K_{S+T}=K_T\circ K_S.
\]
The repository also proves a stronger path-level identity. Cutting a constructed path at an intermediate horizon and concatenating an independent continuation started at the cut state recovers $\Pp_{S+T,x}$ (\lean{pathLawFrom_add}). Taking the terminal pushforward of this path-level theorem yields the kernel semigroup law as a shadow.

\subsection{Finite-dimensional distributions}

Let
\[
  0=t_0\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_n\le T
\]
be a finite family of nondecreasing observation times. The measurable sampling map reads the right-continuous trajectory at these times. The formalization proves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fdd}
  \Pp_{T,x}\circ (X_{t_0},\ldots,X_{t_n})^{-1}
  = \delta_x K_{t_1-t_0}\cdots K_{t_n-t_{n-1}},
\end{equation}
where the right-hand side is the usual Markov product law (the chronological kernel chain of the formal development). For a state sequence $(x_0,\ldots,x_n)$, the atom formula is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fdd-atom}
  \mathbf{1}_{\{x_0=x\}}
  \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}
  \exp((t_{i+1}-t_i)Q)(x_i,x_{i+1}).
\end{equation}
The formalization also proves the general form without the requirement $t_0=0$:
prepending time zero and marginalizing the first coordinate replaces the initial
indicator by the entry $\exp(t_0Q)(x,x_0)$
(\lean{pathLawFrom_finiteDimensional_eq_general},
\lean{pathLawFrom_sampleAt_real_singleton_eq_exp_product_general}), so all
finite-dimensional distributions at nondecreasing observation times are covered.
Thus the semigroup is not merely an API defined beside the path construction: it is identified with the actual terminal and finite-dimensional laws of that construction.

A branching three-state Y-shaped chain instantiates the general theorem. It is the smallest reversible network with a genuine branching choice: from the hub state a jump can reach either leaf, so the process is not a telegraph process, and its path law cannot be reduced to the parity-of-jump-count argument available for a two-state alternating chain.
