\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Stochastic thermodynamics and Markov jump processes}

The fluctuation relations formalized here are classical. Jarzynski established the nonequilibrium work equality and derived it in a master-equation formalism \parencite{Jarzynski1997PRL,Jarzynski1997PRE}. Crooks related forward and reverse path probabilities for microscopically reversible Markovian dynamics and derived both work and free-energy relations \parencite{Crooks1998,Crooks1999,Crooks2000}. Path-space formulations of entropy production for Markov processes were developed by Lebowitz and Spohn, Maes and Neto\v{c}n\'y, and Seifert \parencite{LebowitzSpohn1999,MaesNetocny2003,Seifert2005,MaesNetocnyWynants2008}. Time-dependent finite-state jump processes and detailed fluctuation theorems are standard subjects in stochastic thermodynamics \parencite{ShargelChou2009,RaoEsposito2018,PelitiPigolotti2021}. Our contribution is not a new fluctuation identity, but a machine-checked construction connecting the identities to explicitly constructed path measures.

\subsection{Continuous-time Markov-chain path laws}

The embedded jump chain, exponential holding times, explosion theory, Kolmogorov equations, and $P_t=\exp(tQ)$ are standard \parencite{Norris1997}. Explicit path densities on disjoint unions of finite jump records combine jump-rate factors with integrated escape rates \parencite{RaoTeh2013}. The free-simplex chart and the transition-matrix product are therefore not claimed as new mathematics. The formal contribution is that the normalized sector sum is an actual path law whose terminal and finite-dimensional pushforwards are identified with the matrix-exponential kernels, and that the same law is then used as the window dynamics of a physical driven protocol.

\subsection{Mechanized probability and CTMCs}

Mechanized Markov-process theory predates this work. H\"olzl constructed discrete-time Markov processes and jump-and-hold continuous-time chains in Isabelle/HOL, proving the Markov property, a transition semigroup, and a backward equation; the current Archive of Formal Proofs session (in its \file{Continuous_Time_Markov_Chain} theory) also contains an almost-sure non-explosion result under bounded escape rates \parencite{Hoelzl2017,AFPMarkovModels}. In Lean, Marion formalized the Ionescu--Tulcea theorem in Mathlib, providing general trajectory-measure infrastructure \parencite{Marion2025}.

The closest Lean CTMC comparison is Ripple, which develops finite-state Q-matrices, exponential holding times, matrix-exponential transition probabilities, a canonical Ionescu--Tulcea jump-hold law, and conditional non-explosion results for an infinite record construction \parencite{Ripple2026}. Our work does not claim the first Lean CTMC or jump-hold law. In the Ripple snapshot reviewed for this paper, we did not identify a theorem equating the terminal pushforward of its canonical law with its matrix-exponential transition API, finite-dimensional pushforward identifications, or a stochastic-thermodynamic driven layer. This is a snapshot-specific search result, not a proof of absence.

\subsection{Mechanized fluctuation relations and physics}

Lean has been applied to chemical physics, equilibrium thermodynamics, and canonical ensembles through LeanChemicalTheories and Physlib \parencite{Bobbin2024,Physlib}. Concurrently, the PhysicsAI DFT moment-body development formalizes the detailed fluctuation theorem as a measure identity and derives the integral fluctuation theorem, second-law and moment consequences, and finite-support Crooks pairs \parencite{PhysicsAIDFT}. That development assumes a DFT law and studies its consequences. The present work instead constructs forward and reverse laws from finite-state generators and local Gibbs detailed balance. The two projects are therefore complementary: one analyzes distributions satisfying a fluctuation symmetry, while the other derives the physical symmetry on global path measures built from explicit dynamics.

\subsection{Positioning statement}

To our knowledge, among the public Lean~4 and Mathlib artifacts reviewed at the fixed snapshots recorded in the bibliography (search completed on 26 August 2026 and quality-checked on 27 August 2026), we did not identify a development connecting all of the following in one machine-checked construction: normalized fixed-horizon path measures for finite-state jump generators, disappearance of the finite-horizon jump-count tail, terminal and finite-dimensional identification with the matrix-exponential semigroup, measurable concatenation of finitely many piecewise-constant protocol windows, and global Crooks--Jarzynski work relations. This statement is deliberately limited to the reviewed public artifacts and snapshots. It is not a priority claim over all theorem provers, and none of the underlying physical laws or standard CTMC identities is claimed as new.
